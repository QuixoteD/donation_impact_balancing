Girls in School Initiative

Divide a gift across the programme and see what each part pays for, who it reaches, and what the research says.

Sierra Leone's 13,444 schools enrol about 3.45 million pupils, 1.78 million of them girls (MBSSE, 2025). GISI is Corus International's programme to keep girls in school in Sierra Leone. Wi De Ya, the Ministry of Basic and Senior Secondary Education's school reporting system, profiles every learner, so cash and support go to the girls most at risk. When a girl stops attending, the school is alerted, a case worker visits, and the family is linked to the help it needs. The work runs with the Teaching Service Commission and district education staff. Nine of the thirteen parts below have been delivered in GISI; the other four are options from Corus's programme menu and are marked as such.

About these figures

The page shows what a gift buys and how many pupils it reaches. It does not estimate GISI's own effect on attendance, dropout, or learning: that comes from GISI's evaluation, not from this page. What a gift pays for is an estimate, based on Corus's own costs or, where Corus has not yet costed an activity, on published studies of similar work. Much of the research comes from other countries, and "Why it matters" is Corus's own view. Pupil numbers use Sierra Leone's national averages of about 257 pupils per school, roughly half of them girls, and 37 pupils per teacher (MBSSE, 2025); some pupils are reached by more than one part, so the numbers should not be added together. The page is for illustration and is not an offer or a commitment on how a gift will be spent.
