Girls in School Initiative

Divide a gift across the programme and see what each part pays for, who it reaches, and what the research says.

Sierra Leone's 13,444 schools enrol about 3.45 million pupils, 1.78 million of them girls (MBSSE, 2025). The Girls in School Initiative (GISI), run by Corus International with the Ministry of Basic and Senior Secondary Education, the Teaching Service Commission and district education staff, uses Wi De Ya, the Ministry's school reporting system, to find girls at risk of leaving school and to pay for what keeps them there. When a girl stops attending, the school is alerted, a case worker visits, and the family is linked to the help it needs. Each part below says what a dollar buys. Nine parts have run in GISI; four are priced but have not yet run in GISI, and are marked.

About these figures

Prices are set by Corus and include delivery. The page shows what a gift buys and how many pupils it reaches. It does not estimate GISI's own effect on attendance, dropout, or learning: that comes from GISI's evaluation, not from this page. Much of the research comes from other countries, and "Why it matters" is Corus's own view. Pupil numbers use Sierra Leone's national averages of about 257 pupils per school, roughly half of them girls, and 37 pupils per teacher (MBSSE, 2025); some pupils are reached by more than one part, so the numbers should not be added together. The page is for illustration and is not an offer or a commitment on how a gift will be spent.
